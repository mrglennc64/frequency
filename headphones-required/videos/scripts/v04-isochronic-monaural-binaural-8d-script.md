### DEMONSTRATION
[SCREEN: build binaural — two carriers, split to L/R]

Binaural. Two carriers, one per ear, never mixed. The beat is made in your brain. Headphones mandatory — sum it to mono and it dies. You know this one.

[SCREEN: pre-mix the two tones, play through one channel]

Monaural. Same two tones, but I mix them together *first*, then play the blend to both ears. Now they interfere in the signal itself — you can hear the beat right here, out loud. It's in the file, so it works on speakers. Headphones optional.

[SCREEN: single tone pulsing on/off at 10 Hz, picket-fence spectrogram]

Isochronic. No pair at all — one tone, switched on and off ten times a second. The rhythm's printed right into the waveform. Works on speakers, unmistakable, a bit insistent — good for daytime, bad for sleep.

[SCREEN: "8D" — auto-pan sweeping a source around the field]

And "8D." This is auto-panning — sweeping a sound around your head. Fun effect. But watch what it does to a binaural pair: [SCREEN: the stable L/R carriers smearing as they pan] the carriers that were anchored in each ear are now swinging across the middle, blending. The stable separation — the entire mechanism — is destroyed. [pause] And in the wild, "8D binaural" tracks are usually a *mono* source being spun in a circle. There was never a beat to wreck.

[SCREEN: text card — "'8D binaural' defeats itself."]
