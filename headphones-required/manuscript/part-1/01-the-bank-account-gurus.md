# Ch. 1 — The Bank-Account Gurus
*Part I — The Problem · Target: 7 pp · Write in Week 4 (Jul 27–Aug 2) · Status: not started*

## Purpose & role in the argument
The hook. Establish the market problem the whole book answers: manifestation audio became a get-rich-quick genre run by people with 30 days of experience and a bank-account screenshot. Position Glenn as the whistleblower with 30 years of receipts — without ever sounding bitter. The reader should finish this chapter feeling *seen* (they suspected something was off) and curious, not lectured.

## Key points to hit
- How manifestation audio became a genre: the income-screenshot economy; sellers who show you their results but never their process
- The asymmetry: audio engineering is invisible to the buyer, so the market rewards marketing skill, not construction skill
- The AI-sludge flood of 2023–2026 — generated tracks at scale, indistinguishable from the "handmade" catalogs because on measurement there *is* no difference
- What 30 years of production work looks like by contrast (brief, factual, no résumé-reciting — the receipts come in later chapters)
- Set the book's contract with the reader: everything claimed here is measurable; you'll be given the tools to check it — including on my work

## Reuse from existing assets
- Audit sample, channel-level P2 finding: "the audience has no way to distinguish this catalog from AI-generated content — because on three of six tracks, there is no measurable difference." That sentence is the chapter's thesis in miniature.

## Tone & CTA
Forensic, calm, evidence-first. Anti-sludge, never anti-spirituality — say explicitly that the practice deserves better than its loudest sellers. No CTA; the chapter's job is trust. Forward-reference Ch. 2: "you can test all of this yourself in ten minutes."
