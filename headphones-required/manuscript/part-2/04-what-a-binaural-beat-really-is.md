# Ch. 4 — What a Binaural Beat Really Is
*Part II — How It Actually Works · Target: 8 pp · Write FIRST, Week 1 (Jul 6–12) · Status: not started*

## Purpose & role in the argument
The title chapter and the book's load-bearing wall. Everything else — the lead magnet, the sales page, V2, the audit service — quotes this chapter's central argument. A reader who understands only this chapter can never be sold a mono "binaural" file again.

## Key points to hit
- Two carriers, two ears, one brain: a slightly different pure tone in each ear; the brain's binaural processing produces the perceived beat at the difference frequency (e.g. L 200 Hz, R 210 Hz → 10 Hz beat)
- The beat exists **only in your head** — it is not in the file, not in the air, not recoverable from a speaker. This is neurology, not mixing
- Why headphones are not a suggestion: on speakers the carriers mix acoustically before reaching either ear; the difference signal is destroyed at the point of delivery
- Carrier choice matters: the effect works best with carriers roughly under ~1000 Hz and beat frequencies under ~30 Hz — which is why "528 Hz binaural" claims need scrutiny (bridge to Ch. 7)
- Diagram: the two-carrier construction, what each ear receives, where the beat arises (this diagram recurs in Ch. 8 and the certificate spec)
- Close with the book's namesake rule: if a product doesn't say *headphones required*, it either isn't binaural or its maker doesn't know how it works — both are disqualifying
