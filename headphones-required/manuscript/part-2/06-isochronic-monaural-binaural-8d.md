# Ch. 6 — Isochronic, Monaural, Binaural, "8D"
*Part II — How It Actually Works · Target: 7 pp · Write in Week 2 (Jul 13–19) · Status: not started*

## Purpose & role in the argument
The format taxonomy. After Ch. 4 the reader knows what binaural is; now give them the whole map so no format label can be used against them. Ends with the chapter's set-piece: why "8D binaural" is a contradiction in terms.

## Key points to hit
- **Binaural** (recap in one paragraph): two carriers, headphones mandatory, beat made in the brain
- **Monaural beats:** the two tones mixed *before* playback — the beat is physically in the signal, works on speakers, different (peripheral) mechanism
- **Isochronic tones:** a single tone pulsed on/off at the target rate — the most speaker-friendly format; when it's the right choice and why sellers who don't know the difference default to mislabeling everything "binaural"
- Honest comparison table: format / where the beat exists / headphones? / appropriate use
- **"8D audio":** automated panning for spatial novelty — legitimate as an effect, but panning a source around the stereo field *destroys* the stable L/R carrier separation binaural requires. "8D binaural" is self-defeating by construction
- Decision guide: which format for which situation (speakers at bedtime → isochronic/monaural; focused headphone session → binaural)

Taxonomic and fair — each format gets its legitimate use case stated before its misuse is shown; the villain is mislabeling, not any format. No sales CTA; forward-reference Ch. 8 for how a correct session is built in each format.
