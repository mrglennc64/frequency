# Ch. 7 — The Frequency Myths
*Part II — How It Actually Works · Target: 9 pp · Write in Week 2 (Jul 13–19) · Status: not started · ⚠ FACT-CHECK GATE: historical claims verified before launch*

## Purpose & role in the argument
The myth-busting chapter readers will quote — and the one most likely to draw hostile scrutiny, so every historical claim must be airtight. Separates three layers the genre blurs: what's historical, what's invented, and what's harmless preference vs. false claim.

## Key points to hit
- **528 Hz "DNA repair":** trace the actual origin of the claim (Solfeggio revival literature, not molecular biology); why no plausible mechanism connects an audio frequency to DNA; what 528 Hz actually is (a pitch — a perfectly nice one)
- **432 Hz conspiracy:** the real history of concert pitch standardization (A=440 adopted for tuning consistency, not by conspiracy); historical pitch varied wildly by city and era; preferring 432 Hz is a legitimate aesthetic choice — claiming it's "the universe's frequency" is not
- **Solfeggio origins:** the medieval solmization syllables vs. the modern six/nine-tone "ancient Solfeggio frequencies" — a 20th-century construction retro-projected onto history; date it precisely
- The key distinction, stated as the chapter's framework: **preference is free, claims carry a burden of proof.** Tuning to 432 Hz because you like it: fine. Selling 528 Hz as cellular medicine: fraud-adjacent
- How myths function commercially: a number is marketable ("528 Hz!") in a way that craft ("a clean 60-second entrainment ramp") is not — which is exactly why the sludge market runs on numbers

- Audit sample, "528 Hz Abundance Meditation" and "Chakra Alignment Suite" verdicts: note that the chakra suite *passes* — tones present and pitched as claimed, clean construction — the perfect illustration that honest labeling is possible even for spiritual framing

## Tone & CTA
Most delicate chapter in the book: forensic on claims, generous to believers. Never mock the reader's playlist. No sales CTA; bridge to Part III — "now that you know what's false, here's what correct actually takes."
