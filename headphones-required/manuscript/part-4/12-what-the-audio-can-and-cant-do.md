# Ch. 12 — What the Audio Can and Can't Do
*Part IV — The Practice · Target: 5 pp · Write in Week 6 (Aug 10–16) · Status: not started · ⚠ FACT-CHECK GATE: claims cited or softened*

## Purpose & role in the argument
The results-question chapter. After eleven chapters of exposing overclaims, the book must now make its own claims — and be visibly more careful than anyone it criticized. Defines the defensible territory and cuts the wish-engine framing off at the root.

## Key points to hit
- **Entrainment as a state tool, not a wish engine:** the honest model — audio can nudge *state* (arousal, attention, relaxation); state influences what you do; what you do produces outcomes. The audio is the first link, not the whole chain
- **The defensible territory** (with the Ch. 5 / Appendix C evidence): sleep onset support, relaxation, attention/focus scaffolding — modest, real, worth having
- **The indefensible territory, named plainly:** attracting money, repairing DNA, altering external events — no mechanism, no evidence, and the sellers know it
- Placebo, treated honestly: expectation effects are real and not shameful — but a seller who *relies* on placebo while charging for engineering is selling the label, not the product
- What "it works for me" means and deserves: respect for the experience, honesty about attribution — set up Ch. 13's measurement journal as the personal answer to the attribution question

## Reuse from existing assets
- Ch. 5's evidence framework (don't re-argue — reference and apply)
- The book-wide disclaimer language (educational audio-engineering analysis, not medical/therapeutic advice) gets its substantive treatment here

## Tone & CTA
The most restrained chapter — every sentence written as if a hostile expert will quote it. This restraint *is* the marketing: the guy who spent 200 pages debunking has maximum credibility only if his own claims are bulletproof. No sales CTA; bridge to Ch. 13: "here's how to find out what it does for *you*."
