# Appendix C — Research Reading List
*Target: 3 pp · Assembled continuously from Week 1, finalized at the Week 6 fact-check gate · Status: not started · ⚠ This appendix IS the fact-check gate's output*

## Purpose & role
Actual studies, honestly summarized *including limitations*. This appendix is the book's spine of receipts: every research claim in Ch. 5, 7, and 12 must trace to an entry here, and every entry must state what the study does NOT show. A reading list with disclosed limitations is something no bank-account guru has ever published — it is itself a differentiator.

## Entry format (fixed, per study)
- Citation (authors, year, journal, DOI/link)
- What was tested (design, N, duration)
- What was found (effect, size if reported)
- **Limitations** (sample size, replication status, funding, placebo control) — mandatory field
- Which chapter cites it

## Collection buckets (fill while drafting the chapters — do not defer to Week 6)
- **Binaural-beat perception mechanics** (Ch. 4): carrier/beat frequency ranges over which the percept exists
- **Entrainment outcome studies** (Ch. 5, 12): attention, relaxation, sleep onset — include null results and meta-analyses, not just positive findings
- **Subliminal persuasion evidence** (Ch. 9): the weakness of the effect is the point — cite the strongest available review
- **Historical sources** (Ch. 7): concert-pitch standardization history; the documented modern origin of "ancient Solfeggio frequencies"; origin of the 528 Hz claims
- **Placebo/expectation effects** (Ch. 12): honest framing sources

## Working rules
- A claim with no entry here gets softened or cut at the Week 6 gate — no exceptions
- Prefer meta-analyses and reviews; single small-N studies get flagged as such in the summary
- Every summary written so a hostile expert would call it fair

## Tone
Librarian, not advocate. The limitations field is where the book's honesty is most checkable — write it as if the study's own authors were reading.
