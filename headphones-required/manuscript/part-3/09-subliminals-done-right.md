# Ch. 9 — Subliminals Done Right
*Part III — The Craft · Target: 7 pp · Write in Week 4 (Jul 27–Aug 2) · Status: not started*

## Purpose & role in the argument
The chapter that handles the genre's second-biggest product category with the same forensic fairness as binaural: what a competently mixed affirmation layer looks like, why most "subliminals" are theater, and the ethics of the whole enterprise. The −52 dB demonstration is the set piece (also V7).

## Key points to hit
- What a subliminal layer actually is in production terms: a voice layer mixed below conscious attention but **above perception threshold**, under a masking bed
- Masking thresholds in practice: the correct working range is a consistent −18 to −24 dB offset beneath the bed — quiet enough to not draw attention, present enough to exist perceptually
- **Buried-beyond-perception is theater:** a layer at −52 dB relative to the bed is below any plausible perception threshold — indistinguishable from silence. The customer paid for words that functionally aren't there; demonstrate with phase inversion (how the audit isolates the layer)
- Consistency matters: an offset that wanders defeats masking design; loudness-normalize the final master
- **The ethics section (do not cut):** even done right, what's the honest claim for subliminals? The evidence for subliminal persuasion is weak — say so. The defensible product is transparency: published affirmation script, documented offset, listener consent to the content. Anything hidden-by-design deserves suspicion — that's the line between craft and manipulation
- What to demand from any subliminal seller: the script, the offset, the construction doc
